RevOps

Should you hire a RevOps person or outsource it?

By the Afaao team · November 12, 2026 · 5 min read

At some point every growing B2B team hits the same wall. The CRM is messy, reports take a day to build, leads fall through the cracks, and the people fixing it are a sales manager and a marketer doing it on the side. Someone says we need a RevOps person. The real question is whether that should be a hire, a freelancer or an outside team.

Short answer

Hire in house when you have enough ongoing ops work for a full time role and need someone in every meeting. Outsource when the work is real but part time, covers several tools, or comes in bursts like a cleanup or migration. Many small B2B teams start outsourced and hire once the workload is steady.

What the work actually is

Revenue operations covers the systems and process behind marketing, sales and customer success: the CRM, marketing automation, lead routing, data quality, integrations and reporting. Some of it is steady upkeep. Some of it is project work that comes in bursts, like a migration, a cleanup or a new scoring model. Which mix you have matters more than your headcount.

Option 1: hire in house

  • Good at: deep knowledge of your business, always available, sits in the meetings where decisions get made.
  • Falls short when: there is not enough work to fill the role, one person is expected to be an expert in every tool, or they leave and the knowledge goes with them.
  • Best for: teams with steady daily ops work across sales, marketing and CS, usually once the go to market team is a few dozen people.

Option 2: a freelancer

  • Good at: a single well defined job in one tool, like building a set of workflows or fixing a sync.
  • Falls short when: the work spans several systems, needs someone to own it over time, or the freelancer is not available when something breaks.
  • Best for: one off tasks with a clear start and end.

Option 3: an outsourced RevOps partner

  • Good at: covering several tools and skills without several salaries, scaling hours up for a project and down afterwards, and bringing patterns from other teams.
  • Falls short when: you need someone embedded full time, or nobody internally has time to make decisions and answer questions.
  • Best for: small and mid sized B2B teams where the work is real but part time, or where a big cleanup has to happen before a hire would make sense.

Five questions that settle it

  1. How many hours of ops work do you really have each week? List the requests from the last two months and add them up.
  2. How many tools are involved? One person rarely knows the CRM, marketing automation, enrichment, routing and a CS platform equally well.
  3. Is the work steady or in bursts? Bursts favour outside help, steady favours a hire.
  4. Who will own decisions internally? Outside help still needs one person who can say yes or no.
  5. What happens if this person is away for a month? If the answer is everything stops, you need documentation or a team, not a single point of failure.

A common path for small teams

Many teams start with outside help to clean up the foundation and set standards, keep a small monthly block of hours for upkeep, and hire in house once the workload is steady enough to fill a role. By then the systems are documented, so the new hire starts with a clean setup instead of inheriting a mess.

Whichever you choose

  • Write down how your funnel works on one page before anyone builds anything.
  • Insist on documentation for every workflow, field and integration.
  • Agree on three to five reports that define success, and review them monthly.
FAQ

Quick answers

When should a startup hire its first RevOps person?

When there is enough steady ops work to fill most of a week and the cost of slow or wrong data is hurting sales. Before that, part time or outsourced help is usually enough.

Can outsourced RevOps work alongside an in house admin?

Yes. A common setup is an in house admin handling daily requests while an outside team takes projects such as migrations, integrations or reporting rebuilds.
