How to fix a messy HubSpot portal or a failed HubSpot implementation

By the Afaao team · November 10, 2026 · 6 min read

A lot of HubSpot portals get set up in a hurry. Someone imports a spreadsheet, a few workflows get built for a launch, an agency leaves, and two years later nobody is quite sure what anything does. Reports do not match, sales has gone back to spreadsheets, and the question becomes whether to rebuild from scratch. Most of the time you do not need to. You need a calm, ordered cleanup.

Short answer

You rarely need to rebuild a messy HubSpot portal. Pause new changes, agree on your process, audit workflows and properties, then fix in order: data, lifecycle stages, ownership and routing, pipelines, and automation. Finish by rebuilding a few reports the team trusts.

Signs the implementation has gone wrong

  • Sales and marketing give different numbers for the same thing, like MQLs or pipeline.
  • Lifecycle stages are wrong for a big share of contacts, or nobody agrees what they mean.
  • There are dozens of workflows and nobody knows which ones are still needed.
  • Properties have duplicates like Industry, Industry 2 and Company industry.
  • Reps keep their own spreadsheets because the CRM feels unreliable.
  • Leads sit without an owner or get assigned to people who left.

Step 1: stop making it worse

Before fixing anything, pause new workflows, new properties and new imports unless they are urgent. Agree who can make changes for the next few weeks. Cleanups fail when people keep building on top of the mess while it is being fixed.

Step 2: write down how the business actually works

HubSpot should reflect your process, not the other way round. Spend an hour with sales, marketing and CS and agree on a few basics: what counts as a lead, when it becomes an MQL and an SQL, who owns it at each step, and what your deal stages really are. Keep it to one page. Every later decision gets checked against it.

Step 3: audit before you delete

Export lists of workflows, properties, forms, lists and pipelines. For each one, note whether it is used, what depends on it, and who owns it. HubSpot shows property usage and workflow enrollment history, which makes this much faster. Mark items as keep, fix or retire. Do not delete anything yet.

Step 4: fix the foundation first

Work in this order, because each layer depends on the one before it.

  1. Data: merge duplicates, standardise key fields like country, industry and job title, and archive junk contacts that will never be marketed to.
  2. Lifecycle stages and lead status: set clear rules and fix existing records in bulk.
  3. Ownership and routing: make sure every new lead gets an owner, with a fallback.
  4. Pipelines: simplify deal stages to match how you really sell, with required fields only where they matter.
  5. Automation: rebuild or retire workflows based on the audit, and name them clearly.

Step 5: rebuild trust with a few reports

Pick three to five reports leadership actually uses, such as new leads by source, MQL to SQL conversion, pipeline by stage and closed won by month. Make sure each one matches reality, then retire the rest. When people see the numbers are right, they start using the CRM again.

Step 6: keep it clean

  • Name things consistently, for example Team, purpose and date in every workflow name.
  • Keep a short change log and require a reason for new properties.
  • Review duplicates, unassigned leads and failing workflows monthly.
  • Train the team on the few things that matter most to them, not every feature.

When starting over does make sense

A full rebuild is rare. It can be worth it when the data is mostly unusable, the portal was built for a completely different business model, or you are moving to a new HubSpot account anyway. Even then, the audit and the one page process document from steps 2 and 3 are what make the new setup succeed.

FAQ

Quick answers

How long does a HubSpot cleanup take?

It depends on the size of the portal and how much data needs fixing. Small to mid sized portals are often stabilised in a few weeks when the work follows a clear order: process, audit, data, then automation.

Should we hire an agency or fix HubSpot in house?

If someone in house has protected time and HubSpot experience, many fixes can be done internally. Outside help is useful for the audit, the data cleanup and setting standards, especially when nobody owns the portal day to day.
