HubSpot lifecycle stages: how to set them up properly

By the Afaao team · November 23, 2026 · 6 min read

Lifecycle stages are the backbone of HubSpot reporting. When they are set up well, you can see how many leads became customers and where they stalled. When they are not, every funnel report is wrong and sales and marketing argue about the numbers.

Part of our HubSpot consulting guides.

Short answer

HubSpot lifecycle stages track where a contact or company is in your funnel: Subscriber, Lead, Marketing Qualified Lead, Sales Qualified Lead, Opportunity, Customer, Evangelist and Other. Set clear rules for each stage, automate the moves with workflows, keep the date stamps, and let stages move forward only.

The default stages

HubSpot comes with these stages for contacts and companies. You can rename them or add your own, but most B2B teams do best keeping close to the defaults and writing clear rules for each.

  • Subscriber: signed up for your blog or newsletter only.
  • Lead: showed some interest, such as downloading a guide or filling out a form.
  • Marketing Qualified Lead: fits your target and showed enough intent for sales.
  • Sales Qualified Lead: sales has reviewed and accepted them as worth pursuing.
  • Opportunity: a deal is open.
  • Customer: a deal is closed won.
  • Evangelist: a happy customer who refers or reviews.
  • Other: anyone who does not fit, such as partners or vendors.

The hardest line to draw is between MQL and SQL. We cover that in detail in MQL vs SQL.

Rules that keep stages trustworthy

  1. Write one sentence for each stage that sales and marketing both agree on.
  2. Move contacts forward with workflows, not by hand. For example, a closed won deal sets the contact and company to Customer.
  3. Let stages only move forward. HubSpot is built that way by default. If someone needs to go back, clear the property on purpose in a workflow and log why.
  4. Keep lifecycle stage and lead status separate. Lifecycle stage is where they are in the funnel. Lead status is what sales is doing with them right now.
  5. Sync the company and contact stages so an account and its people tell the same story.

Use the date stamps

HubSpot records the date each record entered each stage. These dates power funnel and velocity reports, such as how long it takes an MQL to become an opportunity. Never bulk import over them by accident, and if you migrate from another CRM, keep the original dates in their own properties as we describe in our Pipedrive to HubSpot migration checklist.

Common mistakes

  • Every form fill becomes an MQL, so sales stops trusting the stage.
  • Old imports set everyone to Lead, including customers.
  • Nobody moves contacts to Customer, so marketing keeps nurturing paying clients.
  • Stages are changed by hand, so reports drift month by month.
  • Lead status values do not match how sales actually works a lead.

A quick setup checklist

  1. Agree on one line definitions for every stage.
  2. Build workflows for each forward move, starting with MQL and Customer.
  3. Fix existing records in bulk, starting with customers.
  4. Route every new MQL to an owner. If that breaks, see why HubSpot leads go unassigned.
  5. Build one funnel report and check it against reality every month.

FAQ

Quick answers

Can a HubSpot lifecycle stage move backwards?

By default HubSpot only moves lifecycle stages forward. To move a record back you clear the property first, usually in a workflow, then set the new stage.

What is the difference between lifecycle stage and lead status?

Lifecycle stage shows where someone is in the overall funnel. Lead status shows what sales is doing with them right now, such as new, attempting to contact or connected.
