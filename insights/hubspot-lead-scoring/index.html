HubSpot lead scoring: how to set it up so sales trusts it

By the Afaao team · November 24, 2026 · 7 min read

Most HubSpot portals we look at have a lead score. Very few have one that sales actually uses. The difference is rarely the tool. It is whether the score was built from real closed deals and agreed with the people who are meant to act on it.

Part of our HubSpot consulting guides.

Short answer

HubSpot lead scoring gives each contact or company a number based on two things: fit, meaning how well they match your ideal customer, and engagement, meaning what they have done, like visiting pricing or opening emails. Build the score from data you trust, set a threshold that sales agrees means ready, and review it every quarter against deals that actually closed.

Fit and engagement, scored separately

HubSpot's lead scoring tool lets you build fit scores and engagement scores, and combine them. Keep them separate in your head even if you combine them later. Fit answers whether this is the kind of company and person you sell to. Engagement answers whether they are paying attention right now. A perfect fit with no engagement is a nurture target. Heavy engagement from a student is not a sales lead.

Start from closed won deals, not guesses

  1. Pull the last 30 to 50 closed won deals and their contacts.
  2. Note what they had in common: industry, company size, country, job title, seniority.
  3. Look at what they did before becoming an opportunity: pricing page visits, demo requests, webinar attendance, replies.
  4. Do the same for closed lost and for leads that never converted, so you can see what does not predict a sale.

This turns scoring from opinion into evidence and makes it far easier to get sales to agree.

Building the fit score

  • Give the most points to the two or three traits your best customers share, often industry and company size.
  • Subtract points for clear bad fits such as competitors, students or personal email domains.
  • Only score properties that are filled in reliably. If job title is empty on half your contacts, enrichment comes first.
  • Use company properties for company level fit so every contact at a good account benefits.

Building the engagement score

  • High intent actions such as demo requests, pricing page visits and replies to sales emails should carry the most weight.
  • Low intent actions such as email opens should carry little or none, since privacy features inflate opens.
  • Set limits so one person reading ten blog posts does not look like a buyer.
  • Use decay so activity from six months ago counts for less than activity this week.

Setting the threshold

Pick a threshold, then check it against history: how many contacts would have crossed it last month, and how many of those became opportunities. If sales would be flooded, raise it. If good deals never cross it, lower it or adjust the points. When a contact crosses the line, a workflow can set the lifecycle stage to MQL, which ties into HubSpot lifecycle stages and the difference between an MQL and an SQL.

Common mistakes

  • Too many criteria. Ten well chosen rules beat fifty.
  • Scoring form fills the same no matter which form. A demo request and a newsletter signup are not equal.
  • No negative scoring, so bad fits drift up over time.
  • No owner and no review, so the score slowly stops meaning anything.
  • A score with nowhere to go. If crossing the threshold does not assign an owner, nothing happens. See why HubSpot leads go unassigned.

Review it every quarter

Compare the scores of contacts on recently closed won deals with everyone else. If winners do not score clearly higher, the model needs work. A short quarterly review with sales keeps the score honest.

FAQ

Quick answers

What is the difference between fit and engagement scoring in HubSpot?

Fit scoring measures how closely a contact or company matches your ideal customer. Engagement scoring measures what they have done recently, such as visiting your pricing page or requesting a demo.

What is a good lead score threshold?

There is no universal number. Set a threshold, then test it against last month's contacts and see how many would have become opportunities. Adjust until sales gets a manageable number of leads that actually convert.
