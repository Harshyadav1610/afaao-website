Do you need a full time Gainsight admin? Your options for a growing CS team

By the Afaao team · October 21, 2026 · 5 min read

You bought Gainsight to help your CS team see risk early and run a consistent playbook. Six months later the health score is out of date, CTAs pile up unanswered, and the person who set it up has moved on. The question most teams end up asking is whether they need a full time Gainsight admin. The honest answer is that it depends on how much change your setup needs every month.

Part of our Gainsight consulting guides.

Short answer

Not always. Large CS teams with constant change requests benefit from a full time Gainsight admin. Smaller teams, roughly 2 to 30 CSMs, are often better served by shared ownership with protected time or a part time partner. Count the hours your requests need each month before deciding.

What a Gainsight admin actually does

It helps to separate the work into two buckets. The first is keeping things running: data loads and connectors, user access, fixing broken rules, small changes to scorecards, CTAs and reports. The second is building new things: new playbooks, journey programs, a new segment, a renewal process, or a cleaner health score. Most teams need a steady trickle of the first and occasional bursts of the second.

Most of this work sits inside Gainsight consulting and admin support, and a lot of it starts with the health score, which we cover in building a Gainsight health score your team trusts.

Option 1: hire a full time admin

This makes sense when you have a large CS team, several Gainsight modules in use, lots of integrations, and a backlog of requests that never shrinks. A dedicated admin learns your business deeply and is always available.

  • Best for larger CS organisations with constant change requests.
  • Watch out for: Gainsight admins are a specialised and in demand skill, hiring can take months, and one person is a single point of failure when they take leave or move on.

Option 2: give it to someone already on the team

Many teams hand Gainsight to a CS Ops person, a Salesforce admin, or the most technical CSM. This works for keeping things running if that person has real time set aside and some training. It usually breaks down when the job is a side task. Building gets pushed back, small issues add up, and the CS team slowly stops trusting what they see.

  • Best for small teams with a simple setup and someone who genuinely enjoys the platform.
  • Watch out for: no protected time, no backup, and knowledge that lives in one person's head.

Option 3: a part time partner

A consultant or small firm can cover both buckets without a full salary. You get a set number of hours each month for running the platform, plus help with bigger projects when you need them. A good partner also documents what they build, so you are not locked in.

  • Best for growing CS teams of roughly 2 to 30 CSMs that need Gainsight to work properly but do not have enough work for a full time hire.
  • Watch out for: response times and availability. Agree on how quickly requests get picked up and who your contact is.

A simple way to decide

  1. List every Gainsight request from the last three months, including the ones nobody got to.
  2. Estimate the hours each one would take. Be honest about the backlog.
  3. If that adds up to more than about three weeks of work a month, a full time admin is worth considering.
  4. If it is a few days a month with occasional projects, shared ownership with good documentation or a part time partner is usually a better fit.
  5. Whatever you choose, write down how the platform is set up: data sources, rules, scorecards and who owns what.

Signs your setup needs attention now

  • CSMs keep their own spreadsheets because they do not trust the health score.
  • CTAs are piling up and nobody closes them.
  • Data loads or the CRM connector fail and nobody notices for days.
  • New CSMs get access weeks after they start.
  • Leadership asks for renewal and risk reports that take hours to build by hand.

If two or more of these sound familiar, start with a short audit before deciding on headcount. Fixing the foundation often reduces how much ongoing admin time you need in the first place.

FAQ

Quick answers

How many hours a month does Gainsight admin work take?

It varies with team size, modules and integrations. Small, stable setups may need a few hours a week, while large programs with many integrations can need one or more full time people. Tracking your requests for a few months gives the most reliable answer.

Can a Salesforce admin also manage Gainsight?

Often yes for the basics, especially if Gainsight connects to Salesforce. Gainsight has its own concepts like rules, scorecards and journey orchestration, so plan time for training or outside help for bigger changes.
