Lead routing: a practical guide for HubSpot and Salesforce teams

By the Afaao team · November 24, 2026 · 7 min read

Lead routing sounds like a small admin task until leads start sitting with nobody, two reps call the same company, or a big customer's new contact lands with a junior rep. Routing is where marketing's work becomes sales pipeline, and it is one of the most common things we fix.

Part of our RevOps consulting guides.

Short answer

Lead routing is the set of rules that decides which person owns a new lead and how fast they get it. Good routing matches leads to existing accounts first, then routes by territory, segment or round robin, always has a fallback owner, and alerts the rep right away. Speed matters: the longer a lead waits for an owner, the less likely it is to turn into a meeting.

What good routing does

  • Every lead gets an owner, every time.
  • Leads from existing customers and open deals go to the person who already owns the account.
  • New leads are shared fairly, by territory, segment or rotation.
  • The owner is alerted within minutes, not hours.
  • Anything unexpected lands in a queue a real person checks every day.

The order of the rules

  1. Clean the lead first: normalise country, company name and domain so the rules can match.
  2. Match to an existing account by email domain or company. If there is an owner, route there.
  3. Route by segment or territory, for example by region, company size or industry.
  4. Within a team, use round robin, weighted by capacity if reps carry different loads.
  5. Fall back to a watched queue or a named person when nothing matches.

Order matters because the first rule that matches usually wins. Put the most specific rules first and the catch all last.

Round robin done well

Simple rotation is fair on paper but ignores holidays, ramping reps and people who are already overloaded. Better setups skip reps who are out of office, cap how many new leads someone receives a day, and weight the rotation so senior reps get larger accounts. If a lead comes back months later, consider sending it to the rep who worked it before.

Speed to lead

Routing that is correct but slow still loses meetings. Alert the owner instantly by email, Slack or a task, set a clear expectation for first contact, and report on time to first touch each week. If leads wait for a nightly sync or a manual review, fix that before tuning anything else.

Routing in HubSpot

HubSpot can rotate leads natively and set owners through workflows. Teams usually add company matching so contacts follow the account owner, and a check for contacts with no owner. Our guides on why HubSpot leads go unassigned and lead to account matching in HubSpot cover the details.

Routing in Salesforce

Salesforce uses one active lead assignment rule with ordered entries, plus Flow or a routing tool for round robin and account matching. The most common gap is integrations that create leads without running the rule. See Salesforce lead assignment rules for how to set it up.

When HubSpot and Salesforce are both in play

Decide which system owns routing and stick to it. If HubSpot sets an owner and Salesforce reassigns it on sync, reps lose trust fast. Our guide to HubSpot and Salesforce sync errors explains how owner fields behave across the integration.

Measure it

  • Leads with no owner, checked daily.
  • Time from lead created to owner assigned, and to first activity.
  • Leads per rep per week, to catch an unfair rotation.
  • Leads routed to the wrong team and sent back.

FAQ

Quick answers

What is lead routing?

Lead routing is the process and set of rules that assigns each new lead to the right owner, such as a sales rep, team or queue, as quickly as possible.

What is the best lead routing method?

Match leads to existing accounts first, then route by territory or segment, use a capacity aware round robin within each team, and always keep a fallback queue that someone checks daily.
