How to build a customer health score in HubSpot

By the Afaao team · November 10, 2026 · 6 min read

Not every CS team needs a dedicated platform like Gainsight on day one. If your customers, tickets and renewals already live in HubSpot, you can build a useful health score there. The trick is the same as anywhere else: a few signals people understand, data you trust, and a clear action when the score drops.

Part of our HubSpot consulting guides.

Short answer

Build a customer health score in HubSpot on the company record. Pick a handful of signals such as product usage, support tickets, engagement, feedback and renewal date, give each one points, add them into a score out of 100, and create a task when an account drops into an at risk band.

Two ways to build it in HubSpot

Depending on your plan, HubSpot's Service Hub includes customer success tools with built in health scoring. If you have that, start there. If not, you can build your own score with properties, workflows and a score or calculated property on the company record. Check your current plan in HubSpot to see which tools you have.

Either way, the score belongs on the company record, because renewals and risk are about the account, not one contact.

This sits next to our Gainsight health score guide, which covers the same ideas on a dedicated CS platform. If you want help building it, see our HubSpot consultant page or our work in customer success operations.

Pick a handful of signals

  • Product usage: logins or active users, sent to HubSpot from your product or a data tool.
  • Support: open tickets, high priority tickets, and time to resolve from Service Hub.
  • Engagement: last meeting or email with the customer and whether a champion is still active.
  • Feedback: NPS or survey responses collected in HubSpot.
  • Commercial: days to renewal, overdue invoices or a downgrade request.
  • CSM sentiment: a simple green, yellow or red property the CSM updates.

Get the data in

Support, email and survey data are already in HubSpot. Product usage usually is not. Common options are a direct integration from your product, a reverse ETL or data tool, or a scheduled import into custom company properties. Start with one or two usage numbers you can refresh daily or weekly. A simple signal that is up to date beats a complex one that is a month old.

Turn signals into a score

Give each signal points, for example up to 30 for usage, 20 for support, 20 for engagement, 15 for feedback and 15 for sentiment. Add them into a total out of 100, then set bands such as 70 and above healthy, 40 to 69 at risk, and under 40 critical. Keep the rules written down where the team can see them.

Make it lead to action

  • Create a task for the owner when a company drops into at risk or critical.
  • Add the score and the reason to the company record sidebar so CSMs see it first.
  • Build a view of at risk accounts with renewals in the next 90 days.
  • Review red accounts in your weekly CS meeting.

Check it against reality

Every quarter, look at customers who churned or downgraded and what their score was a few months before. If many were green, a signal is missing or weighted wrong. Adjust once a quarter, not every week, so the team learns to trust it.

When to move to a dedicated CS platform

HubSpot works well for smaller teams and simpler motions. Teams often look at tools like Gainsight, ChurnZero, Totango or Vitally when they need deeper product usage data, many scorecards for different segments, complex playbooks, or journey programs at scale. If that is where you are heading, building a clean score in HubSpot first still pays off, because the same signals and rules carry over.

FAQ

Quick answers

Does HubSpot have a built in customer health score?

Some Service Hub plans include customer success tools with health scoring. On other plans you can build a score yourself with company properties and workflows. Check your plan in HubSpot for what is included.

What is the most important signal in a health score?

For most software companies product usage and relationship strength predict renewals best, but test it against your own churn history every quarter.
