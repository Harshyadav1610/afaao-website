What is RevOps? Revenue operations explained for growing B2B teams

By the Afaao team · November 24, 2026 · 6 min read

RevOps shows up in job titles, LinkedIn posts and vendor pitches, often meaning slightly different things. Underneath the buzzword is a simple idea: the people who buy, sell to and keep your customers should work from the same systems, the same data and the same definitions.

Part of our RevOps consulting guides.

Short answer

RevOps, or revenue operations, is the team and practice that runs the systems, data and processes behind marketing, sales and customer success as one connected engine. Instead of three separate ops teams with their own tools and numbers, RevOps owns the CRM, routing, data quality, integrations and reporting across the whole funnel.

RevOps in one paragraph

Revenue operations brings marketing operations, sales operations and customer success operations under one roof. It owns the tools and processes that move a customer from first touch to renewal: the CRM, marketing automation, lead routing, enrichment, integrations and the reports leadership uses to run the business. The goal is one funnel with one set of numbers.

What a RevOps team actually does

  • Systems: owns the CRM and the tools around it, such as HubSpot, Salesforce, Gainsight, enrichment and routing tools.
  • Process: defines lifecycle stages, MQL and SQL rules, handoffs and deal stages that every team follows.
  • Data: keeps records clean, deduplicated and enriched so reports can be trusted.
  • Routing: makes sure every lead and account has the right owner fast.
  • Reporting: builds pipeline, funnel, attribution and retention reports from one source of truth.
  • Enablement: documents how things work and trains teams on the few things that matter.

RevOps vs sales ops vs marketing ops

Sales ops traditionally supports the sales team: territories, quotas, the CRM and forecasting. Marketing ops supports marketing: automation, lead scoring, campaigns and attribution. CS ops supports customer success: health scores, renewals and platforms like Gainsight. RevOps does not erase these skills. It connects them so that a decision in one team does not quietly break another, for example a new marketing form that leaves leads unassigned in sales.

Signs you need RevOps

  • Marketing, sales and finance report different numbers for the same thing.
  • Leads wait hours for an owner or fall through the cracks.
  • Every team bought its own tools and nobody owns how they connect.
  • The CRM is something people work around rather than in.
  • Leadership cannot answer simple questions like which channel brings the best customers.

When to build it, and how

Most companies feel the need once they have a few people in sales and marketing and more than one or two tools. Early on, one person or an outside partner can cover it part time. As the team grows it usually becomes a full time role and then a small team. We compare the options in should you hire a RevOps person or outsource it.

Where to start

  1. Write your funnel on one page: stages, definitions and who owns each step.
  2. Clean the data that every report depends on.
  3. Fix routing so every lead gets an owner, see why HubSpot leads go unassigned.
  4. Connect the tools that need to share data, and decide which system wins for each field.
  5. Build three to five reports leadership will actually use.

FAQ

Quick answers

What does RevOps stand for?

RevOps stands for revenue operations: the function that aligns the systems, data and processes behind marketing, sales and customer success.

What is the difference between RevOps and sales ops?

Sales ops focuses on the sales team. RevOps covers marketing, sales and customer success together, so the whole funnel runs on shared systems and definitions.
